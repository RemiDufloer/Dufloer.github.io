About the Survey

We are researchers at the University of Lille, France, studying how developers handle a specific bug scenario.

This survey aimed to:

  • Assess debugging strategies in the developer community.
  • Identify resolution strategies and challenges.
  • Understand developers' needs regarding debugging tools and resources.

Your participation was valuable, and we sincerely thank everyone who took the time to complete the survey.

Data Handling Policy

  • Participants' identities remain anonymous; IP addresses are not saved.
  • No sensitive information was collected; participants were asked to avoid sharing personal data.
  • Participants could withdraw at any time by closing the questionnaire; incomplete responses were removed.
  • The survey was hosted on LimeSurvey, and data is stored securely for research purposes only.
  • Responsible researcher: Steven Costiou (steven.costiou@inria.fr), Univ. Lille, Inria, CNRS, Centrale Lille, UMR 9189 CRIStAL, F-59000 Lille, France.

Survey Results

Publication: The survey results and research findings are published in How Developers Perceive Differential Debugging: an Exploratory Survey (DEBT 2026, Brussels). Paper (PDF) · Slides (PDF)

The survey is now closed. Thank you to all 212 developers who took the time to participate and share their experiences. Your contributions made this study possible.

When developers modify a program to add features or refactor existing code, they may inadvertently introduce bugs, resulting in a broken version. In such situations, referred to as differential debugging scenarios, developers need to understand why two versions behave differently in order to identify and fix the underlying problem.

To assess the perceived impact of differential debugging scenarios, we conducted a preliminary study through a survey involving 212 developers from diverse professional backgrounds and application domains. The results show that these scenarios are widespread: 93% of respondents reported having encountered this type of bug at least once. Moreover, more than half of the participants indicated that such scenarios affect their development activities, often requiring considerable time and effort to resolve.

Our findings also reveal mixed perceptions regarding the adequacy of existing practices and tools. While preventive techniques such as continuous integration and code review are generally considered helpful, many developers report that current debugging tools provide limited support for understanding the causes of failures introduced between software versions.

Overall, this survey provides an initial, large-scale characterization of developers' perceptions of differential debugging scenarios. It highlights the need for further empirical research into how such bugs are handled in practice and how tool support could be improved.
